Section 2: Innovations in ThermalX

1. Temperature Gradient Quiver Field

Calculates spatial derivatives ∂T/∂x and ∂T/∂y using central differences. In accordance with Fourier's Law (q = -k∇T), glowing arrows indicate the physical direction of heat escaping from hot CPU/GPU dies towards the cold perimeter.

2. Isothermal Contour Boundaries

Marching squares algorithm extracts lines of constant temperature (35°C, 50°C, 65°C, 80°C). Proves visually that heat flux vectors are strictly orthogonal (perpendicular) to isotherms.

3. 1D Cross-Section Thermal Slice

An interactive horizontal cutline allows the user to slice the 2D field at any row y and plot the continuous 1D temperature profile T(x), verifying steep drops at die edges and exact adherence to 25°C boundary walls.

4. Thermal Shock & Gradient Stress

Automatically calculates the peak gradient magnitude |∇T|_max. Flags regions of severe thermal shock (>10°C/cell) where mechanical expansion poses failure risk in hardware silicon packaging.

Section 3: Top Viva Voce Questions & Answers
Q1: Why does Gauss-Seidel converge twice as fast as Jacobi?

Answer: For the discrete 5-point Laplacian stencil on a rectangular grid, the iteration matrices satisfy Young's Theorem: ρ(G_GS) = [ρ(G_J)]². Because the spectral radius ρ < 1, squaring it cuts the error factor, doubling the asymptotic rate of convergence: R_∞ = -ln(ρ). Thus, Gauss-Seidel reaches the tolerance threshold in approximately half the iterations.

Q2: How do you guarantee that the iterative solvers won't diverge?

Answer: By the Gershgorin Circle Theorem, convergence is unconditionally guaranteed if the coefficient matrix is diagonally dominant. In our 5-point stencil, the diagonal entry is 4, while the sum of off-diagonal neighbors is 4 (and strictly 3 at boundary adjacent cells). This establishes irreducible diagonal dominance, guaranteeing spectral radius ρ < 1 and unconditional numerical stability.
